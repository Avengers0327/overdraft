/# FULL ROUND SCRIPTS — All 22 Subtopics
## Every screen beat, timed, ready to build from

Format per round: **[FIRST LOOK]** (what's shown before commit) → **[YOUR CALL]** (the pick, timed) → **[EVIDENCE]** (stats revealed in order, each with a beat) → **[VERDICT]** (outcome + animation note) → **[CASE NOTES]** (closing line)

## CHAPTER 1 — FRESHMAN YEAR

### Subtopic 1 — Digital currency vs. real money
**Case: "1,000 Gems"**
- FIRST LOOK: Card shows "1,000 GEMS — BEST VALUE" in flashy gold text, price tag "$9.99" small in corner.
- YOUR CALL (8s): Buy the Gem bundle / Skip it and keep the $9.99.
- EVIDENCE:
  1. (flip) "Wanted skin costs 800 Gems" — feels cheap in Gem-terms
  2. (flip) "800 Gems = $7.99 real dollars" — conversion made visible for first time
  3. (flip) "200 Gems left over = not enough for anything" — leftover currency trap shown
- VERDICT: No win/lose stamp — instead a "CASE NOTES ONLY" tag, this round is pure reveal.
- CASE NOTES: *"1,000 Gems sounds like a lot. $9.99 sounds like less. That gap is the whole business model."*

### Subtopic 2 — Needs vs. wants
**Case: "The Case Cover"**
- FIRST LOOK: Cracked phone case (current, free) vs. new $28 case (glossy render).
- YOUR CALL (8s): Buy new case / Keep the $28.
- EVIDENCE:
  1. "Crack doesn't affect function" — confirmed neutral
  2. "New case reviews: 4.2★, common complaint 'peels after 2 months'"
  3. "$28 in a HYSA for 1 year = $29.26" — tiny compounding tease, sets up Chapter 2
- VERDICT: Keeping cash wins on paper — but stamped "NO SHAME" badge appears if player picks the case, avoiding moralizing.
- CASE NOTES: *"Nothing wrong with wanting nice things. Just know which pile you're picking from."*

### Subtopic 3 — Hidden costs of "free" (Chapter 1 Boss)
**Case: "Totally Free"**
- FIRST LOOK: Two game icons — "SkyDash: FREE" (huge free banner) vs. "SkyDash Pro: $4.99" (small price tag, no banner).
- YOUR CALL (10s, boss rounds get slightly longer): Download free / Buy the $4.99 version.
- EVIDENCE:
  1. "Free version average spend: $23/month in power-ups" — first real gut-punch number
  2. "Paid version: $4.99. Forever. That's it."
  3. "12-month cost: Free = $276. Paid = $4.99." — huge visual gap, bar chart snaps up
- VERDICT: Big stamp, "PAID VERSION WINS" — this is Chapter 1's big "wait, WHAT" moment, extra screen shake.
- CASE NOTES: *"Free to download. Not free to enjoy."*

## CHAPTER 2 — FIRST PAYCHECK

### Subtopic 4 — Revenue vs. profit
**Case: "The Lawn Job"**
- FIRST LOOK: "$35/lawn, fewer clients" vs. "$20/lawn, more clients" — both shown as flat weekly income first (deliberately misleading equal-looking numbers).
- YOUR CALL (8s): Pick a pricing strategy.
- EVIDENCE:
  1. "Gas + gear cost: $8/lawn either way"
  2. "Realistic client count this neighborhood: 4 at $35, 9 at $20"
  3. "True weekly profit: $35-tier = $108. $20-tier = $108." — reveals a deliberate tie
- VERDICT: TIE stamp — first round with no clear winner, teaches that the "right" answer isn't always obvious, sets expectation for the whole game.
- CASE NOTES: *"Busy and profitable aren't always the same case. Sometimes they're just... the same number."*

### Subtopic 5 — Banking fees & interest basics
**Case: "The Bonus Trap"**
- FIRST LOOK: "GlitzBank: Sign up, get $10 instantly!" flashy card vs. plain "Riverside Credit Union" card, no bonus mentioned.
- YOUR CALL (8s): Choose a bank.
- EVIDENCE:
  1. "GlitzBank monthly fee: $12" — small print zooms in dramatically
  2. "Riverside monthly fee: $0"
  3. "1-year net: GlitzBank = -$134. Riverside = $0."
- VERDICT: "RIVERSIDE WINS" stamp, GlitzBank card gets a comedic "GOTCHA" rubber-stamp overlay.
- CASE NOTES: *"The bonus cost more than it paid."*

### Subtopic 6 — Savings account types (Chapter 2 Boss)
**Case: "The 0.01% Special"**
- FIRST LOOK: Ugly "Federal Reserve HYSA" card (plain, boring font) vs. sleek "Standard Saver" app card (gradient, modern).
- YOUR CALL (10s): Pick where $1,000 goes.
- EVIDENCE:
  1. "HYSA rate: 4.5% APY" vs "Standard rate: 0.01% APY" — shown side by side, numbers alone don't land yet
  2. "1 year: HYSA = $1,046. Standard = $1,000.10."
  3. "5 years: HYSA = $1,270. Standard = $1,000.50." — bar chart animates the gap widening
- VERDICT: "HYSA WINS — BY A LOT" massive stamp, this is Chapter 2's signature "wow" moment.
- CASE NOTES: *"Filed under: technically a savings account."*

## CHAPTER 3 — THE ALGORITHM

### Subtopic 7 — FOMO/urgency pricing
**Case: "10 Minutes Left"**
- FIRST LOOK: Red countdown timer ticking down live on the card: "09:47 remaining — price goes up after!"
- YOUR CALL (6s — shorter timer here on purpose, to make the player FEEL the manufactured urgency): Buy now / Wait.
- EVIDENCE:
  1. "Same item, same price, last Tuesday — no timer"
  2. "Refresh the page — timer resets to 10:00"
  3. "Item still available at this price tomorrow"
- VERDICT: "WAITING WINS" — timer visual glitches/breaks on reveal, a nice bit of juice showing the fakeness.
- CASE NOTES: *"The clock was never real."*

### Subtopic 8 — Subscription & free-trial traps
**Case: "Free for 7 Days"**
- FIRST LOOK: "Try Premium FREE for 7 days!" big friendly button, tiny gray text below barely visible.
- YOUR CALL (8s): Start trial / Skip.
- EVIDENCE:
  1. Zoom into the tiny gray text: "auto-renews at $14.99/mo"
  2. "Cancel path: Settings > Account > Subscriptions > Manage > Cancel > Confirm" — shown as an absurdly long breadcrumb trail
  3. "Average user cancels: month 4" — silhouette of a forgetting player
- VERDICT: "SKIP WINS" — unless player has previously earned the "Calendar Reminder" bonus unlock, in which case Trial is shown as viable too (small branching path, optional depth).
- CASE NOTES: *"Free for 7 days. Remembered on day 94."*

### Subtopic 9 — Passive vs. active income
**Case: "Post vs. Deal"**
- FIRST LOOK: "$200 one-time sponsored post, paid today" vs. "Evergreen video, ad revenue trickling in."
- YOUR CALL (8s): Which income would you rather have?
- EVIDENCE:
  1. "One-time: $200. Done."
  2. "Passive: $8/month... for now" — starts small, unimpressive on purpose
  3. Time-lapse reveal: "Month 12: passive total = $340 and still earning" — the slow build pays off visually
- VERDICT: Depends-on-timeframe stamp, "BOTH VALID — read the fine print" — deliberately not a clean win, teaches nuance.
- CASE NOTES: *"One pays now. One keeps paying. Both are real income."*

### Subtopic 10 — Upsell/checkout manipulation (Chapter 3 Boss)
**Case: "The Upsell Maze"**
- FIRST LOOK: Shopping cart, base item, "$24.00 — Continue to checkout"
- YOUR CALL: This round has THREE mini-calls in sequence instead of one, mimicking a real checkout flow:
  1. "Add Protection Plan? +$8" — accept/decline (4s each)
  2. "Add Gift Wrap? +$5" — accept/decline
  3. "Upgrade to XL for just $2 more?" — accept/decline
- EVIDENCE: Running total ticks up live in the corner after each accepted upsell — no separate evidence phase, the checkout IS the evidence.
- VERDICT: Final total reveal: "Started at $24. You're at $61." if all accepted, vs "$24. Still $24." if all declined — direct side-by-side of both paths shown post-hoc for contrast regardless of player choice.
- CASE NOTES: *"Nobody forced you. That's the whole trick."*

## CHAPTER 4 — TRUST ISSUES

### Subtopic 11 — Phishing recognition
**Case: "You Won!"**
- FIRST LOOK: Pop-up card, confetti graphic, "YOU'VE WON A FREE PRIZE! Claim now →"
- YOUR CALL (8s): Claim it / Close the pop-up.
- EVIDENCE:
  1. "Company name: 'PrizeHubOfficial' — 0 search results"
  2. "To claim: enter card info for '$4.99 shipping'"
  3. "Real free prizes: never require payment info. Ever."
- VERDICT: "CLOSE IT WINS" — the confetti graphic deflates/pops if player claims, comedic negative feedback.
- CASE NOTES: *"Congratulations, you won nothing."*

### Subtopic 12 — Fake investment pitch recognition
**Case: "Guaranteed Returns"**
- FIRST LOOK: DM screenshot styled card: "bro this is guaranteed, 20% every week, trust me 🚀"
- YOUR CALL (8s): Send money / Ignore.
- EVIDENCE:
  1. "Sender account created: 3 days ago"
  2. "'Guaranteed' + 'every week' = matches 94% of known scam scripts"
  3. "Real index fund average: 7-10% per YEAR" — direct scale contrast, the gap is visually enormous
- VERDICT: "IGNORE WINS" — zero variance on this one, always the same outcome, deliberately.
- CASE NOTES: *"If it's guaranteed and it's 20% a week, it's not investing. It's a costume."*

### Subtopic 13 — Social engineering awareness
**Case: "Just This Once"**
- FIRST LOOK: Chat bubble from "Jordan_2011" (online friend, 2 weeks): "hey can u send a $50 gift card, i'll pay u back i promise 🙏"
- YOUR CALL (8s): Send it / Say no.
- EVIDENCE:
  1. "Known Jordan_2011 for: 14 days, never video called"
  2. "Pattern match: 'gift card + promise to repay' — common script"
  3. "Tracked outcomes of similar asks: repaid 6% of the time"
- VERDICT: "SAY NO WINS" — gentle, not alarmist framing throughout.
- CASE NOTES: *"Real friends don't need gift cards to prove it."*

### Subtopic 14 — Layered/disguised scams (Chapter 4 Boss)
**Case: "The Brand Deal That Wasn't"**
- FIRST LOOK: DM styled like an official brand account: "Hi! We'd love to sponsor your content. Confirm your details here →"
- YOUR CALL (10s): Click confirm / Verify first.
- EVIDENCE:
  1. "Real brand deals arrive via: verified email, business inquiries form — never cold DM"
  2. "The 'confirm' link asks for: password + payment info" — red flag zoomed in
  3. Side-by-side: "What a REAL brand deal DM looks like" shown for contrast, first time the game shows a positive example instead of only red flags
- VERDICT: "VERIFY FIRST WINS" — Chapter 4's closing boss, slightly harder because it looks the most legitimate of all Chapter 4 cases.
- CASE NOTES: *"Real brands don't need your bank password to send you free stuff."*

## CHAPTER 5 — BORROWED TIME

### Subtopic 15 — Minimum payment trap
**Case: "Minimum vs. More"**
- FIRST LOOK: "$600 balance. Minimum payment: $18/mo" vs "Pay $36/mo instead"
- YOUR CALL (8s): Pick a payment plan.
- EVIDENCE:
  1. Month-by-month mini-animation, balance line for both plans races across a chart
  2. "Minimum plan: paid off in 41 months, total paid $738"
  3. "Double plan: paid off in 18 months, total paid $648"
- VERDICT: "PAY MORE WINS" — the racing chart visual is the payoff here, watching the minimum-payment line crawl.
- CASE NOTES: *"Minimum payment. Maximum patience required."*

### Subtopic 16 — Interest rate comparison
**Case: "Two Loans"**
- FIRST LOOK: "QuickCash App — friendly mascot, 5-star rating" vs. "First National — plain, corporate, 3-star rating"
- YOUR CALL (8s): Pick a lender for the same $500 loan.
- EVIDENCE:
  1. "QuickCash APR: 29%" (buried in app's terms page, shown zoomed)
  2. "First National APR: 15%"
  3. "Total repayment: QuickCash = $645. First National = $575."
- VERDICT: "FIRST NATIONAL WINS" despite worse star rating and worse vibes — explicit lesson that branding ≠ terms.
- CASE NOTES: *"The nicer app had the meaner number."*

### Subtopic 17 — Gross vs. net pay
**Case: "First Real Paycheck"**
- FIRST LOOK: "You worked 20 hours at $15/hr = $300 expected"
- YOUR CALL: No choice this round — first "investigation only" case, player taps "Open Case" to proceed.
- EVIDENCE:
  1. "Federal tax withheld: -$24"
  2. "State tax withheld: -$9"
  3. "FICA (Social Security + Medicare): -$23"
  4. "Actual deposit: $244"
- VERDICT: No win/lose — "CASE SOLVED" stamp instead.
- CASE NOTES: *"The government was never hiding. It's just quiet about it."*

### Subtopic 18 — Good debt vs. bad debt (Chapter 5 Boss)
**Case: "Financing the Flex"**
- FIRST LOOK: "Used Honda Civic, $4,800, financed" vs. "Limited sneakers, $480, financed" — same $110/month payment shown for both, deliberately equalized to remove obvious "expensive vs cheap" bias.
- YOUR CALL (10s): Which financing decision looks smarter?
- EVIDENCE:
  1. "Car value in 3 years: still ~$2,900, still drives to work/school"
  2. "Sneakers resale in 3 years: ~$60, if kept mint"
  3. "Miss a payment — car: repossession risk. Sneakers: bad debt mark only, keep the shoes"
- VERDICT: "CAR WINS AS DEBT" stamp, but with a secondary softer note.
- CASE NOTES: *"Nothing wrong with wanting the shoes. Everything wrong with a 24-month loan for them."*

## CHAPTER 6 — CASHED OUT (FINAL)

### Subtopic 19 — Compound growth
**Case: "Boring vs. Hyped"**
- FIRST LOOK: "Index Fund — average, unexciting" vs. "MoonCoin — friend says it's 'literally guaranteed to 10x'"
- YOUR CALL (10s): Where does your $1,000 go?
- EVIDENCE:
  1. "10-year index fund history: steady climb, ends at ~$2,100"
  2. "MoonCoin volatility chart: wild spikes and crashes shown animating chaotically"
  3. Randomized final result (weighted ~85% index wins, ~15% MoonCoin wins) — genuine variance, not scripted
- VERDICT: Weighted random outcome, revealed with real suspense since it's not fixed.
- CASE NOTES (index wins): *"Boring won again. It usually does."*
- CASE NOTES (MoonCoin wins, rare): *"It actually worked this time. Don't get used to it."*

### Subtopic 20 — Risk & diversification
**Case: "All In vs. Spread Out"**
- FIRST LOOK: "$1,000 all in one stock" vs. "$1,000 spread across 5 assets"
- YOUR CALL (8s): Pick your strategy.
- EVIDENCE:
  1. Dramatized market dip animation: single stock chart plunges -40% in one quarter
  2. Diversified portfolio chart dips only -9% same quarter
  3. "Could the single stock have gone up 40% instead? Also yes."
- VERDICT: "DIVERSIFIED WINS ON RISK" — explicitly not "wins on returns," a nuanced stamp.
- CASE NOTES: *"You could've gone all-in and won bigger. You could've also lost it all. That's the whole case."*

### Subtopic 21 — Self-employment tax
**Case: "Nobody Told Me"**
- FIRST LOOK: "$2,000 earned freelancing" vs "$2,000 earned at a W-2 job" shown as equal paychecks first.
- YOUR CALL: Investigation-only case, no pick.
- EVIDENCE:
  1. "W-2 job: taxes already withheld automatically all year"
  2. "Freelance income: self-employment tax owed, ~15.3%, nobody withheld it"
  3. "Surprise bill due in April: $306"
- VERDICT: "CASE SOLVED" — no winner, pure reveal, mirrors Subtopic 17's structure intentionally (bookend design).
- CASE NOTES: *"The IRS doesn't care that nobody warned you. Now you know."*

### Subtopic 22 — Synthesis: evaluating complex real-world offers (FINAL BOSS)
**Case: "The Opportunity"**
- FIRST LOOK: A slick pitch deck card: "Join our team! Recruit 3 friends, everyone earns, guaranteed $500/week, limited spots — financing available to get started!"
- YOUR CALL (12s, longest timer in the game): Join / Decline.
- EVIDENCE (four-stage reveal, each stage exposing one layered red flag, callbacks to every prior chapter):
  1. "Earnings depend on recruiting others, not selling a product" — pyramid structure flag (callback: Ch. 2 profit vs. revenue)
  2. "'Financing available' = you take on debt to join" — debt trap flag (callback: Ch. 5)
  3. "'Limited spots' — same spots have been 'limited' for 8 months" — fake urgency flag (callback: Ch. 3)
  4. "'Guaranteed $500/week' — no business guarantees income" — fake investment flag (callback: Ch. 4)
- VERDICT: "DECLINE WINS" — biggest stamp animation in the game, screen briefly shows a checklist of all 4 flags catching if the player correctly identified them pre-reveal (bonus Instinct points for early reads).
- CASE NOTES: *"Every case you've ever opened led here. Nice work, detective."*

## BUILD NOTES

- Rounds with a fixed timer under 8 seconds should never contain more than 3 evidence beats — pacing breaks if the reveal outlasts the tension from the pick.
- Subtopics 17 and 21 are intentionally structured as twins (both "investigation only," both paycheck-shock reveals) — this repetition is deliberate reinforcement, not laziness, and should be preserved in implementation.
- Subtopic 19 is the only round in the base game with genuine random variance in outcome — everything else is deterministic. This is intentional and should not be "balanced" away; it's the moment the game tells the truth about risk instead of a clean lesson.
- Subtopic 10's three-mini-call structure is more complex to build than any other round — budget extra dev time here, or consider simplifying to two upsells instead of three if time is tight in Chapter 3.
