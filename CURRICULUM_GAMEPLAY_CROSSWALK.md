# CURRICULUM → GAMEPLAY CROSSWALK
## How the 12-unit lesson plan maps onto verticals, tiers, and archetypes
## — and where it currently doesn't.

Companion to `LESSON_PLAN_CURRICULUM_ONLY.md`. That doc is the curriculum on
its own terms. This one is the translation layer: which game system carries
each unit, at what point a player actually encounters it, and what's missing.

---

## PART 1 — THE MAPPING PRINCIPLE

The curriculum is sequenced **chronologically by life stage** (allowance →
bank account → online purchases → fraud exposure → income → credit →
investing).

The game is sequenced **by net worth tier**, with archetype weighting on top.

These are not the same ordering, and that's mostly fine — a player climbing
tiers does roughly recapitulate a financial life. But they diverge in two
places that matter, flagged in Part 3.

Three game systems carry the curriculum:

| System        | Curriculum job                                                                       |
|---------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Vertical**  | Owns a unit (or part of one). The thematic home of a concept.                        |
| **Tier**      | Controls *when* a player meets the concept, and at what scale/complexity.            |
| **Archetype** | Controls *how often* — biases frequency, never excludes (1-in-6 coverage guarantee). |

## PART 3 — THE TWO REAL PROBLEMS

### 3.1 Two of the first four units still have no vertical on the board

Units 1-2 now have a home: **Everyday Money is live** (registered as the open,
Tier-1 foundation — it owns the run's on-ramp and keeps a permanent floor share of
the draw). Units 3 and 4 still don't: Digital Traps and Trust & Fraud aren't
registered yet.

This remains the largest gap between the lesson plan and the build. A player right
now CAN encounter Units 1-2 content (digital currency illusion, bank fee literacy,
HYSA vs. standard savings — all live in Everyday Money), but still can NEVER
encounter: urgency pricing, subscription traps, checkout upsells (Unit 3 — Digital
Traps), or phishing, fake investment pitches, social engineering (Unit 4 — Trust &
Fraud). (Credit vs. debit was never scripted and remains unbuilt.)

**Unit 4 (Trust & Fraud) is the most serious of these** — the lesson plan
explicitly flags it as the most urgent, most actively-targeted content for
this age group, and places it early on purpose. It is currently absent
entirely.

### 3.2 The curriculum's early units are gated behind late tiers

Even once those verticals exist, there's an ordering conflict:

| Unit              | Curriculum position    | Design doc tier gate   | Conflict |
|-------------------|------------------------|------------------------|----------|
| 3 — Manipulation  | Early (urgent)         | Digital Traps, Tier 2+ | Minor    |
| 4 — Trust & Fraud | Early (most urgent)    | Trust & Fraud, Tier 3+ | **Real** |
| 9 — Credit & Debt | Mid (age 15 relevance) | Borrowed Money, Tier 4 | Moderate |

A Tier 3 gate on fraud content means a player must reach $1,000+ in-game
before meeting any scam-detection material. That directly contradicts the
lesson plan's stated rationale for placing it at Unit 4.

**Recommendation:** register Trust & Fraud as an **open (Tier 1)** vertical,
not Tier 3. Scale the *dollar amounts* in its Cases by tier as usual — a
$30 gift-card ask at Tier 1, a $3,000 fake investment at Tier 5 — but never
gate *access* to the pattern-recognition lesson behind net worth. The skill
doesn't require wealth to be relevant; arguably the opposite.

Same logic applies to Digital Traps (Unit 3) — subscription traps and fake
urgency target broke teenagers at least as hard as wealthy ones.

Note the same reasoning already got applied to Insurance & Risk Transfer,
which is live as **open** on the board despite being designed as Tier 3+.
That was the right call, and it's the precedent for doing the same to
Trust & Fraud and Digital Traps.

### 3.3 Two units have no owner even in design

- **Unit 7 (Budgeting & Opportunity Cost)** — currently only touched
  incidentally (Need vs. Want, Rent vs. Buy's opportunity-cost framing).
  No vertical owns it. Either needs a home vertical, or needs to be
  explicitly designated as a *cross-cutting* unit that every vertical
  teaches implicitly rather than one owning it.
- **Unit 12.1-12.3 (compound growth, diversification, fat tails)** —
  Startup World covers equity risk and burn rate, but not the core
  investing literacy. This was the Markets & Risk vertical in the design
  docs; it isn't on the board.

## PART 4 — HOW TIERS CARRY THE SAME UNIT AT DIFFERENT DEPTHS

The strongest part of the mapping: a single unit can be taught repeatedly at
escalating sophistication, because tier controls scale *and* complexity.

Worked example, Unit 2.2 (bank fees), across tiers:

| Tier | Scenario                                            | What's actually taught              |
|------|-----------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------|
| 1    | $10 bonus, $2/mo fee, $50 balance                   | Read the fee                        |
| 3    | $5-25 bonus, $6-15/mo fee, $500 balance             | The bonus can be a trap — sometimes |
| 5    | $500 "relationship bonus," $45/mo fee, hidden AUM % | Sophistication ≠ safety             |

Same unit, three genuinely different lessons. This is the mechanism that
lets 12 units sustain a long session without repeating themselves, and it
should be applied deliberately to every evergreen unit — not just left to
the Cases that happen to have tier-scaled numbers already.

## PART 5 — ARCHETYPE EFFECT ON CURRICULUM DELIVERY

Archetypes bias frequency, never access:

| Archetype    | Over-delivered units                               | Under-delivered (still guaranteed) |
|--------------|----------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------|
| Creator      | 6, then 12.4-12.5 as Startup fades in              | 5, 9, 11                           |
| Entrepreneur | 5, then 12.4-12.5 (+ 6 role-reversed at high tier) | 6, 9, 11                           |
| Athlete      | 4.5 + 12.4 via Sports, then Startup                | 5, 6, 9, 11                        |

The **1-in-6 coverage guarantee** is what keeps "under-delivered" from
becoming "never delivered." Its most important job is ensuring every player,
regardless of archetype, reliably meets Units 4 and 9 — the fraud and debt
content — since no archetype currently majors in either.

## PART 6 — WHAT TO FIX, IN ORDER

1. **Register Trust & Fraud as an open (Tier 1) vertical.** Highest
   curriculum priority; currently entirely absent.
2. ~~**Register Everyday Money as open.**~~ **DONE** — live as the open, Tier-1
   foundation (on-ramp + permanent floor). Carries Units 1-2.
3. **Register Digital Traps as open.** Carries Unit 3.
4. **Decide Unit 7's home** — own vertical, or formally designated
   cross-cutting.
5. **Register Markets & Risk (Tier 4-5)** to carry Units 12.1-12.3.
6. **Find a home for Unit 10.1** (gross vs. net pay) — likely Everyday
   Money or Trades & Hustles.
7. The **coverage guarantee is already built** (the hard outside-pair floor:
   ≥1 in every 6 non-foundation draws comes from outside the player's archetype
   pair). It will automatically protect Trust & Fraud / Digital Traps the moment
   they're registered — so items 1 and 3 are all that stand between the plan and
   the fraud/manipulation content reliably reaching every player.
